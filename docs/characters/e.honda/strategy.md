# E. Honda Strategy

## Neutral, S3 updated

Whilst Honda is not quite as reliant on a single normal move such as Lily's 2HP, a lot of E.Honda's power is in standing Medium Punch.

This move does a lot for E.Honda. It is an excellent poke, it is an excellent keepout tool, a whiff punish tool, and a pressure starter, as well as being special cancellable. It moves E.Honda forward, giving him deceptively large range for a medium poke. Unfortunately, Honda suffers a little because it is slightly slow, at 10 frames to start up. Nevertheless, it is a key tool that you must use if you want to be successful with E.Honda.

Honda has some weaknesses in neutral. Notably, the range where other characters like Ken and Cammy have excellent crouching medium kicks.

This area is the range E.Honda generally wants to avoid. You do not have a crouching medium to contest at the tips of these ranges, so you must play further, at 5MP tip range, or closer, letting you threaten command grab and frametraps. He has some counterplay at this range, with his standing medium kick (unavailable in Modern), but the reward for landing a counterhit, or even a punish counter, 5MK is not high. Do your best to avoid this range, and manoeuvre closer, or further away.

Contrary to popular opinion, and changed from season 1 to 2, Sumo Slam and Headbutt are tools you must use sparingly! Headbutt is not an approach tool - use it as a keepout tool to stop dashes and preemptively stop drive rushes, as well as any other forward movement. E.Honda does get good reward off of landing a Headbutt in neutral, however, on block, he is put into a mixup scenario very close to the opponent. This is especially dangerous versus Zangief and other grapplers, who would love to get a free +3 scenario. Sumo Slam also has great reward on hit, and you are +2 , but the spacing makes it hard to use the frames, and it is best used as a read for a fireball or as a mixup to jumping in.

Honda's primary weakness is his lack of a cancellable low move. He can't pressure with a drive rush cancelled 2MK! This means that when you enter the area where other characters have 2MK, you need a plan.

Your plan can be very simple but the idea is that you must not stay in this zone for very long.

These are some of the examples to deal with The Danger Zone, but as you get better at playing the game and Honda, you will find that there are many solutions to the 2MK game that you build your repetoire.

3HK can be kara-canceled during startup frames 15~16. Off any +23 knockdown, an immediate DR~3HK will cause any reversal Super to occur during the kara-cancel window. This allows Honda to perform his own Super during the opponent's freeze. This will lose to any wakeup option that is 9 frames or faster (including Throws), and trade with 10-frame normals (often with slight advantage for Honda).

Similar tech can be employed from blocked pokes like 5MP &gt; DRC~3HK in cases where the opponent uses a Super to blow through your blockstring gap (a common technique called Drive Rush Option Select or DROS). This will generally allow Honda to beat the opponent's Super with his own kara-canceled SA1 (if not a projectile) or SA3, both of which have the same invincibility window. Knockdown Advantage: all scenarios assume that opponent will Back Rise midscreen when possible, unless otherwise stated

Sumo headbutt and sumo smash create two of Honda's fundamental offensive scenarios.

Against a grounded opponent, headbutt is +40 on hit.

As an example sequence, you can do the following against a cornered opponent: headbutt knockdown, 5MK (frame kill), 5MP, 2P, 2P, medium headbutt.

If they wake up by pressing a button or jumping, 5MP will combo into 2P, letting you combo into headbutt. If they wake up with DI, you can cancel 5MP or 2P into your own DI. If they block the entire sequence, you will have dealt approximately 2 drive bars of damage.

Against an airborne or juggled opponent, headbutt is +41 or higher on hit. When the opponent is +42, +43, or +44, Honda gets a safe jump.

With Season 2, your opponent cannot back roll after a hit sumo slam. This opens up a huge world of options for Honda, as you are +23 oB frames and right in their face.

#### Basic sequences

Sumo headbutt and sumo smash create two of Honda's fundamental offensive scenarios.

Against a grounded opponent, headbutt is +40 on hit.

As an example sequence, you can do the following against a cornered opponent: headbutt knockdown, 5MK (frame kill), 5MP, 2P, 2P, medium headbutt.

If they wake up by pressing a button or jumping, 5MP will combo into 2P, letting you combo into headbutt. If they wake up with DI, you can cancel 5MP or 2P into your own DI. If they block the entire sequence, you will have dealt approximately 2 drive bars of damage.

Against an airborne or juggled opponent, headbutt is +41 or higher on hit. When the opponent is +42, +43, or +44, Honda gets a safe jump.

With Season 2, your opponent cannot back roll after a hit sumo slam. This opens up a huge world of options for Honda, as you are +23 oB frames and right in their face.

## Modern Solutions

Whereas most characters in SF6 are significantly weaker in Modern, E. Honda is relatively unique in that both Classic and Modern are equal in power, with each control scheme offering different strengths and weaknesses. Modern Honda gives up some of Classic's offensive prowess and explosive potential in exchange for stronger defenses and neutral tools.

Utlizing the special button allows Modern Honda to safely buffer a reversal OD headbutt while in blockstun. Classic Honda often struggles under pressure due to requiring giving up charge to attempt his reversal, many times having to make a hard read about where the opponent will leave a gap in their pressure. Modern Honda never has to stop charging and can attempt to armor through strings as often as he likes. This also allows Modern Honda to perform an imitation of the DP OS characters with invincible uppercuts have against Drive Rush pressure.

For Classic Honda, SA1 is primarily relegated to a wake-up reversal and (admittedly high damage) combo ender, but Modern Honda can fully utilize this move's properties turning it into a 1600 damage half screen counterpoke in neutral and whiff punish on defense. Normally Honda struggles to properly capitalize in +7 situations due to the slow speed of his medium buttons, but the lightning fast speed of this super combined with its range allows him to strike back where Classic Honda wouldn't be able to act.

Despites its stated purpose being an anti-fireball tool, a myriad on complicating factors prevents Ultimate Killer Head Ram from being able to effectively function as such. A charge requirement, relatively slow startup and an awkward motion input all combine to make for a super that rarely manages to punish fireballs on reaction, even while looking for it. While Modern Honda still needs charge, the instantaneous reaction ability Modern allows him to reliably punish projectiles against most of the cast.

## Modern Struggles

Although the tools provided by Modern are very powerful, they are not without costs. Although the main page lists what tools M Honda lacks, it doesn't detail the implications of not having access to those buttons. Although none of these on their own are a huge loss, in aggregate they reduce Honda's ability to open up opponents and neuter his ability to snag sudden kills. That said, in 95% of cases, reduced combo damage is not something Modern Honda has to worry about. Outside of specific circumstances he has access to all the same routes as Classic without incurring Modern scaling.

Possibly the most significant change in that Honda can only use his command grab through the special button, and he can only use H or OD Oicho. Modern Honda's H Oicho ends up being weaker than Classic's L Oicho while having significantly less range, while spending 2 bars of drive only bumps up Modern OD Oicho to being slightly stronger than Classic medium command grab. Combined with Oicho's lackluster oki on a successful grab, the risk-reward skews significantly out of Honda's favor and defangs his Overhead/Throw mixups.

Modern Honda only has access to his overhead through his Toko Shizume target combo. Normally, Honda doesn't mind this much, the extremely telegraphed nature of the move makes it difficult to use it in neutral or out of drive rush as other characters might use their overhead. Where lacking the raw overhead hurts is in oki situations where Classic Honda can set up a meaty stomp or threaten a kara cancel into Oicho or Buttslam, Modern doesn't have this option. Furthermore, he doesn't have access to any of the Season 3 Stomp combos, which include a strong safejump via 236K~2P, 3HK, [4]6MP/HP. Realistically, stomp routes are only useful in 2-3 drive jump-in combos and specific corner combos where Modern Honda can find routes that deal slightly less damage.

Honda's standing light kick is a somewhat lackluster tool on its own. In most situations where you would want to press a 4 frame button, 2LP is likely the better choice. However, 5LK also happens to be the button with the lowest total duration of any of Honda's attacks, which makes it useful in a variety of frame kill situations. It also combines with Honda's slow Drive Rush to allow him to brake almost instantly out of a drive rush to bait opposing checks in neutral or reversals on oki. That said 5LK is more of a "nice to hav" than a necessity in those situations. Often there's a roughly equivalent setup for oki where you'd use 5LK and DR whiff throw performs similarly well for baiting reactions. Outside of this 5LK has a few other niches in clap combos and as an abare tool, but nothing that Modern Honda will really find himself hurting for.

Theoretically, 5MK is a tool for Honda to beat lows. But the kick's horrendous speed combined with the narrow window where the high profile is in effect and its low reward on hit make it a rather poor button overall. After buttslam, Honda can use a Drive Rush 5MK as a Drive Reversal safe meaty, but otherwise, Modern Honda isn't really missing much not having this move.
