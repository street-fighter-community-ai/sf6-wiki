# Alex

## Overview

A young wrestler considered to be the next big thing. He debuted with his adoptive father Tom as his trainer, but was quickly made a heel due to his perceived surliness.

Debuting in Street Fighter III and returning from SFV, Alex is once again a grappler specializing in burst movement and relentless pressure. Where Manon's gameplan is about mixups, Lily’s about stock based plus frames, and Zangief's is about landing the SPD, Alex instead is a high-aggression, high mobility brawler who specializes at getting into medium-close range and forcing your opponent into constant guesses with relentless frame advantage, attacks that hold your Drive Gauge hostage, and big damage once he lands a clean hit.

Alex’s pressure on knockdown is some of the most suffocating offense to deal with in Street Fighter 6. He can approach above, behind, low, or the front, and each has a variety of options, attributes, and follow ups. Armored attacks that leave him plus, command grabs, and sneaky resets -- and that’s only scratching the surface.

Alex's buttons are long reaching and strong, giving him good pokes that allow him to roll into his game plan. 5MK has incredible range for harassing enemies, 5HK is excellent as a big punish and trap tool, and 5HP has an utterly gargantuan disjointed hitbox to win poke wars. Both of the standing heavies can be charged too, which gives him better frame data and unique properties (like the ability to special cancel from Charged 5HP). 5MP and 2MP are his go-to cancelable pokes, and his 5LK can be linked after certain moves for longer combo extensions. His 5MP can be followed up with a new Target Combo called Palm Strikes, which creates a safe frame trap on block while setting up Prowler Stance oki if it knocks down.

Condition them to block with Flash Axe, his brand new light and medium variant of the powerful Flash Chop. Axe is safer on block and easier to combo into, as the powerful Chop only cancels properly on charged 5HP or 2HP. Aerial Knee Smash returns as his anti-air special; the light and medium versions are extremely fast and both give good oki, while the heavy is your ideal combo tool. Power Bomb and Power Drop are Alex's high-damage command throws, and it even retains the Street Fighter III 'back turn' mechanic, where moves that cause your opponent to spin before the throw (such as Heavy Lariat or Flash Chop) pay out better screen positioning and damage.

Brand new to Alex's kit is Prowler Stance, which functions like a more versatile version of Chun Li's Serenity Stream that can be performed in neutral or canceled from specific combo starters. He ducks low to the ground in a low-profile stance, and while he can't block or crouch under projectiles, he gains access to a vast arsenal of normal and special follow-ups. Alex gains a long-range chainable jab called Palm Thrust that cancels into specials or most other Prowler follow-ups. Air Stampede and Slash Elbow are terrific approach tools using both aerial or grounded movement, respectively. Tactical Hop allows Alex to juke OD reversals or sneak in a crafty cross-up at close range. Sweep Combination is a low Target Combo that gives good oki and big damage on Counter-hit. Shoulder Launcher can punish any aerial move on reaction, including jump-ins, safe specials like Sumo Headbutt or Blanka Ball, and even some airborne Supers that would otherwise punish Alex's stance. Heavy Lariat leads to combos on hit or pressure on block; by charging it up, it gains more block advantage and 2 hits of armor that can beat the opponent's Drive Impact, OD reversal, or other attempted counters. When Alex has them conditioned to only block or Parry, he can throw them with Hyper Takedown, which is functionally a normal throw that can be safe if the opponent tries to jump away. From farther away, the leaping command throw Dangerous Armbar can catch standing opponents or blow through any projectile in the game on reaction; it becomes the more devastating DDT if it lands as a Punish Counter, which will make opponents think twice about trying to parry any of Alex's approach options. The sheer variety of offensive weapons at his disposal makes any knockdown incredibly stressful for the opponent.

This incredible oki roulette comes at a cost; Alex's defensive tools are subpar, with no OD reversal and generally slow Supers that can be blocked in many oki setups. He has relatively few cancelable normals, and is notably lacking cancels from low pokes or from safe heavy pokes (except for the highly reactable Charged 5HP), limiting his ability to control the midrange and punish back walks. Power Bomb deals solid damage, but the range drops off dramatically on faster versions, making it much easier to escape some pressure strings. While Prowler Stance is phenomenal, there are limited ways to cancel into it and his movement is limited to dashes, jumps, and attacks while in the stance. He also lacks a standard throw loop, forcing him to either use Hyper Takedown or spend meter on Drive Rush, while his wide hurtbox makes him more susceptible to throw loops himself.

All in all, if you like big damage, relentless offense, or you just really like looking at his muscles, Alex has a 141 ⅔ percent chance of being your character.

### Pick if you like
- STRETCH 'EM: Hard knockdowns and looping pressure in and out of Prowler Stance, letting Alex maintain a terrifying offense.
- Good Workrate: A wide array of tools from command grabs to evasive hops to armored strikes, with even more reward on Punish Counter.
- 20-Inch Pythons: Long-reaching pokes with great reward even at a distance.
- OUTTA NOWHERE!!!: Able to pressure with plus frame attacks like heavy lariat & command grabs to keep opponents guessing from anywhere on the screen

### Avoid if you dislike
- In-Ring Psychology: Recognizing when to use your options from Prowler Stance for maximum reward.
- Getting Cut Off: Poor reversal options barring a raw Super Art.
- Abnormal Throw Loop: Has to use Drive Rush or enter Prowler Stance in order to throw loop properly, telegraphing Alex's intentions more than other characters.
- Just Playing the Heel: No cancelable lows, limiting his back walk punishes.
